In the previous post, I mainly wrote about moving companies, so this time I thought I'd also write about my experience looking for an apartment in Japan.

When I first came to Japan, I lived in a company-leased apartment (借り上げ社宅). Basically, the company signed the contract with the property management company under its own name, and I only needed to choose which apartment I wanted to live in. Because of that, I had absolutely zero experience with finding and renting an apartment in Japan myself. This time, after changing jobs, my new company didn't have this kind of support, so I had to handle the entire rental process myself. Another level unlocked in my survival experience.

## Rental Platforms

There are quite a lot of rental platforms in Japan, but I mainly stuck to a few of the bigger ones — **[SUUMO](https://suumo.jp/), [LIFULL HOME'S](https://www.homes.co.jp/), and [at home](https://www.athome.co.jp/)** — and browsed them day after day. Besides these three, I also checked **ホームメイト**, which was the platform used for my previous company-leased apartment, but the listings weren't particularly different from the other platforms, so I eventually stopped checking it.

Because my new company had a housing allowance with a distance requirement, my search area was narrowed down quite a lot. The upside was that I had a much smaller area to focus on, which made it easier to concentrate on finding a place. Based on my experience this time, **SUUMO** had the largest number of rental listings, although there were definitely quite a few bait listings. Still, I found it very useful in the early stage for figuring out what I wanted and getting a rough idea of rental prices, and its filtering functions were actually pretty good. I first learned about **at home** from recommendations by some bloggers and used its app for a while, but I didn’t find many rental listings that were unique to the platform, so I eventually stopped checking it. And finally, my personal favorite was **LIFULL HOME'S**. A lot of the listings seemed to be genuine, and I also liked its filtering UI.

## Starting the Apartment Hunt

I think figuring out your rental requirements at the beginning is pretty important. I first narrowed down the area based on my company's housing allowance, then browsed a huge number of listings on **SUUMO** to get a rough idea of what kind of apartment I could afford in which areas. After that, I started checking **LIFULL HOME'S** every day for apartments that matched my requirements.

What I was looking for:

- At least 20㎡
- At least 2nd floor
- Separate bathroom and toilet
- Not too old (at least built in the 21st century..)
- Free Internet
- Bicycle parking available
- South-facing balcony
- Concrete structure
- No guarantor required
- Foreigners allowed
- Within budget

I originally also wanted an apartment that came with furniture and appliances, because my previous company-leased apartment already had a bed and appliances, which made moving in super easy. But apparently that was asking for too much. Adding this requirement immediately eliminated about 90% of the options, so I quietly gave up on that one..

## When to Contact Real Estate Agents

I was going to start working at my new company in August and planned to move into the new apartment in late July, so I started looking at apartments as early as May. Whenever I found something I liked, I would save it. After browsing for about a month, I picked out a few apartments I particularly liked and contacted the corresponding agents. Quite a few of them told me that my move-in date was too far away 😅 and that they couldn’t wait that long.

That was when I realized that in Japan, you generally don't need to start contacting agents until about a month before you actually want to move in. So naturally, I waited until June to officially start contacting them...

From what I experienced, rental applications are basically handled in the order they are submitted, which I thought was pretty nice. But that also means that if you really like a place, you should try to apply as soon as the listing goes live.

I contacted a few agents about apartments I was interested in. One agent politely asked me what country I was from and said they would contact me again after the weekend. And then... silence. Classic Japan.. Another agent said they preferred someone who could move in sooner, so that one was also a polite rejection.

For the last apartment I contacted an agent about, the listing had just been posted when I reached out. I asked about the initial costs and a few other things I cared about. The initial costs were honestly pretty painful 😭. In order to avoid becoming homeless on the streets of Tokyo, I thought about it for two days and decided to apply. The agent told me I was the first person to apply, so as long as I passed the screening, the apartment was basically going to be mine.

However, there was one very unpleasant surprise in the middle of the process. The initial estimate was a little over ¥330,000, but after the screening was completed, the official final estimate came out to over ¥370,000. An additional translation fee from the guarantor company had suddenly appeared, even though I didn't need any translation. Needless to say, I was pretty annoyed.

I went back and forth with the agent and battled over it for several rounds, even pulling out the “I might have to reconsider signing the contract” card 😢. They still wouldn't budge and basically said, “If that's the case, there's nothing we can do, so please reconsider.” Seeing that there was no room for negotiation, and not wanting to go through the whole apartment-search-and-screening process again, I could only swallow the loss.

## Signing the Contract

After paying the initial costs, the actual contract process began. Since I wasn't in Tokyo, I chose to sign remotely, which involved an online **IT important matters explanation (IT重要事項説明)** and then receiving the paper contract by mail to sign.

That was basically the whole apartment-hunting and rental process. It took a little over a month from start to finish.

## After Moving In

When I first opened the door with the key, the room was _so much smaller_ than I had imagined. I hereby formally accuse Japanese rental listings of using wide-angle cameras for their photos!

The room was empty and tiny, and that moment of disappointment 😔, combined with having to unpack everything and buy a bed and appliances, was honestly brutal. A bed, refrigerator, washing machine, and all that stuff are basically impossible for one girl to move by herself, so I had to pay someone to install everything. (A small complaint about how unfair this is to women..)

After about three days of chaos, I had basically finished setting everything up, and the new place finally started to look like a home.

Now that I've been living here for almost two months, the disappointment I felt when I first moved in has completely disappeared. After decorating it myself, I've grown to really like the place 😃. Especially the view from the balcony. It doesn't have that cramped, buildings-everywhere feeling that I usually associate with Tokyo. It's surprisingly open, and I really love it 🧡.

## Thoughts

Moving once in Japan really does mean a massive hit to the wallet..

The whole process of finding and renting an apartment is seriously long and tedious..

There really aren't that many apartments in Japan that are willing to rent to foreigners.. and it seems like there are fewer and fewer.

If you have the option to view an apartment in person, I'd definitely recommend doing that first. This time, I wasn't in Tokyo, and the apartment was still occupied by someone else at the time, so I couldn't view it either in person or online beforehand. Basically, I was opening a mystery box.

If you really want to open that mystery box, make good use of **Google Maps** and carefully check the surroundings of the apartment. For example, see if there are elevated roads or major roads that might cause noise, or if there are tall buildings blocking the sunlight, etc.

But overall, I think the quality of housing in Japan is pretty good, and that's something I'm quite happy with.
